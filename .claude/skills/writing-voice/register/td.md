# The TD voice, in brief

Read with `exemplars/td.md`, never instead of it. The source of these observations is `notes/writing_profile/source_register_profile.md`, sections 2 to 5.

The technical description states what a thing is and stops. A term is defined in a declarative sentence, often in a numbered item, and then used by its full name on every later mention, so that two areas are "BAA A" and "BAA B" eleven times in one passage and never "it" or "the other" (TD-03). A design choice carries its reason in the same sentence, with "because", "since" or "so that", and the choice follows under "Therefore," "As such," or "Consequently," (TD-04). A simplification is announced as one ("for simplicity") with what was dropped named (TD-09). A mechanical consequence the reader might miss opens with "Note that" (TD-09, TD-10, TD-11). Scope is stated once and flatly, in the document's own formula, "the scope of ... does not include ..." (TD-05). The network is named as a facility, a constraint, an intertie, an area or a flow contribution, and there is no image for it anywhere in the document.

The verbs are fixed: flow contributes, a constraint binds or materializes, price impacts, revenue accrues, an area bears effects, capacity is reserved or released, a constraint set is considered, ignored, enforced or relaxed (TD-08). The judgment vocabulary is small: the document states difficulty ("very complex") without apology and praises nothing.

Sentences are long by ordinary standards and plain in construction, lists are numbered rather than run into prose, and the connectives do the logical work, so the prose has no beats, no short aphoristic sentences, no closers that restate.

One contrast, in the form Anthropic's own guidance uses for a prose instruction. The mannered writer produces "the constraint acts as a dial the operator can turn"; the technical description produces "the net export transfer may be limited in the IFM by a volumetric constraint" (TD-07). The first names an image and asks the reader to translate it; the second names the constraint. When a literal phrase is available, the document uses it.
